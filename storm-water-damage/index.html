How storm water gets in during the wet months

Wet-month storms in Southern California tend to arrive in bursts, and wind pushes rain sideways at the weak points of a house. Window frames with dried caulk, door bottoms with worn sweeps and sliding doors with clogged tracks are the common entry points. Water runs down the inside of the wall and spreads under baseboards before anything looks wet.

Clogged gutters and downspouts are the second cause. When they overflow, water falls beside the foundation and soaks into soil or runs toward the nearest low spot. A yard graded toward the house will push that water against the wall or garage door.

This page is about water that comes in at the openings and low points. Water entering through the roof has its own page, roof leak water damage cleanup, and rising water from a stream, drain backup or street flood is covered under flood damage restoration.

Where we find storm water and what it tells us

Wet carpet along an exterior wall

Rain has come through a window frame, a door base or the wall itself and traveled under the baseboard. The pad is probably holding more water than the carpet surface shows.

Water pooled in the garage

The threshold or driveway slope let runoff in. Boxes on the floor soak from below, and the bottom of the drywall wicks water upward.

Stained sill and swollen window trim

Water is leaking through the frame, not the roof. The trim and the wall under the window need a moisture reading.

Wet soil against the foundation

Overflowing gutters or low grading are dumping water at the house. The fix outside belongs to a landscaper or contractor, while the dry-out inside is ours.

We mark each wet area with a moisture meter and photograph it. A thermal imaging camera shows damp patches behind paint and under flooring, which helps us decide how far to cut.

Storm water safety in the first hour

Stay out of electrical danger

Do not step into standing water near outlets, a panel or a plugged-in appliance. Storm water near outlets is a reason to call 911 or the utility, and the main breaker is touched only from dry footing.

Keep street water out of the house

If runoff is heading for a door, sandbags or a plastic barrier can slow it. Ask the city where to get sandbags rather than assuming a location.

Treat flood-contaminated water with care

Water that entered from the street, a drain or a yard can carry dirt and sewage. Keep skin, pets and household vacuums away from the water.

Photograph, lift and call

Take dated photos, lift belongings onto dry surfaces and call our 24/7 line. Do not wait for the storm to end if water is still coming in.

No arrival time is promised during a storm, because roads and crews vary, but same-day response is scheduled when possible and we talk you through the first steps by phone.

The first-day plan: debris, sandbags and clearing

Storm cleanup often starts with debris. Leaves, mud and branches that block a driveway drain or downspout will send the next round of rain into the house, so clear what you safely can after the storm passes. Do not reach into a storm drain grate, and leave grate clearing to the city.

The city's 311 line is where LA Sanitation lists service requests, so report a blocked street inlet there. That keeps the public side of the drainage working while you handle your side.

Inside, we sort materials by what the water touched. Clean rain on a hard floor is quick to deal with, but mud-laced water on carpet is a different job. Each material is sorted into dry-in-place or remove, with photos taken of what leaves the house.

How we dry what the storm soaked

Extraction comes first. Pumps and extraction units remove standing water from tile, concrete and carpet, and we extract wet carpet in slow passes. Pad that has been under water or muddy runoff usually comes out.

Then we set the drying system. Air movers cover the wet walls and floors, dehumidifiers sit in each affected room, and doors to dry rooms stay shut so the equipment works on one volume of air. Mat or injectidry systems pull air through wet cavities under floors when baseboards are tight.

Readings are taken each visit and compared with a dry reference reading in an unaffected room. Equipment leaves only when the wet materials match that reference. If drywall at the base of an exterior wall stays wet, we cut a flood cut a few inches above the water line so the cavity can dry.

Storm water, your policy and the 30-day flood wait

The California Department of Insurance says wind and storm damage is generally included in most homeowners policies, and that standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood. Where your loss falls between those two lines depends on how the water entered, so ask your insurer before you decide how to describe it.

Flood policies bought through the NFIP generally need a 30-day wait before they start, so one bought during the storm will not help this time. A policy bought during a storm will not respond to that storm, so the time to ask is before the wet months, and checking the FEMA flood map for your address is a good start.

After a loss, the department advises reporting storm damage promptly, taking photos and keeping records of all cleanup and repair costs. Photos taken the same day, plus our readings, help show what the storm let in.

Storm drains, Machado Lake and Harbor City drainage

LA Sanitation's page for Ken Malloy Harbor Regional Park describes about 290 acres on South Normandie Avenue, including the 45-acre Machado Lake. Its rehabilitation project scope lists treatment systems at major storm drain inlets.

For a homeowner, that tells you storm runoff in this part of the city is handled through public inlets and a managed system, not just the street. If water ponds in front of your house, ask the city about the inlet near you and do not assume it is working.

Mistakes to avoid after a rainstorm

  • Mopping the surface and leaving the carpet pad and baseboards wet.
  • Running a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Opening walls without a moisture reading, which makes more mess than needed.
  • Waiting for the sun to dry the house, when wet materials past about two days start to raise the mold question.
  • Throwing out wet items without photos, which makes a claim harder.

Anything that looks like mold growth is a separate question that we handle under mold remediation practice. Neighbors in Carson, east of Harbor City, and Lomita, west of us, can use the same dispatch line for storm damage.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency storm water damage cleanup available in Harbor City?

Yes. The line at (424) 550-5662 is answered through the night when a storm hits. Tell the dispatcher which rooms are wet and whether water is still coming in.

Rain came under my front door and the carpet is soaked. Do I need a crew?

Usually, yes. The pad holds water that the carpet surface hides, and wet baseboards and drywall wick it upward. A moisture reading tells you whether the dry-out is small or needs equipment.

How is this different from flood damage restoration?

This page covers rain that gets in at windows, doors, the garage and low spots. Rising water from a stream, drain backup or street flood is handled under flood damage restoration, where water categories matter more.

Does my homeowners policy cover storm water damage?

It depends on how the water entered and on your policy. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

Can I get sandbags before a storm?

Ask the city where sandbags are available in your area, since we do not supply them. Sandbags can slow runoff at a doorway or garage, but they do not seal against water.

Is rain damage in a Harbor City garage worth drying?

Yes, if the walls or stored items got wet. Garage drywall wicks water upward, and cardboard boxes hold it against the slab. Photograph everything first, then call us for a reading.
