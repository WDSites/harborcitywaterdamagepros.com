When water is spreading in Lomita

Act on the source before anything else. In Lomita the first step can depend on where the leak sits, because the city handles its own meters.

Find the right valve

If the leak is inside, close the shutoff at the house. If it is at the meter, only city staff may shut off water at city meters, so ring the Water Division at 310-325-9830 or the after-hours water line at 310-597-6428.

Cut power to wet rooms

Switch off breakers for wet areas only if the panel is dry and reachable without stepping in water. If water is near the panel, stay out and call the utility or 911 first.

Move the small stuff up

Get shoes, paper, rugs and electronics onto counters or beds. In close-set Lomita blocks, a minute spent here saves flooring and baseboards on the other side of the wall.

Keep clear, then call us

Keep people and pets out of any water that came from a drain or toilet. Once the flow has stopped or been reported, call (424) 550-5662 and our 24/7 line will plan extraction.

The problems a small, dense city sees most

Four situations account for most calls from a compact city like this, and each needs a different first move. Name yours before you phone anyone.

Supply line or fixture failure

A braided hose, angle stop or water heater connection lets go and soaks a closet or bath. The water starts clean, so fast emergency water removal beats drywall wicking it upward.

Upstairs or next-door overflow

In attached housing, water travels down through ceilings and across shared walls. We open the cavity and dry the wall system, a job closer to water mitigation than mopping.

Sewer backup

The city owns the sanitary sewer, so report a backup to City Hall at 310-325-7110. Stay out of the water, and leave cleanup to water damage cleanup crews.

Storm runoff at doors and garages

Heavy rain can send water toward thresholds. That is flood-type water, handled under flood damage restoration.

Who controls the water: Lomita Water Division and Cal Water

Lomita's water is city-owned. The Water Division runs the distribution system and the Cypress Water Production Facility, and it serves 4,242 service connections.

Cal Water states that its Palos Verdes Peninsula system serves a portion of Lomita, so confirm who bills your water before you call anyone. Your bill names the provider, and that decides which number you ring first.

Storm drains, sandbags and the harbor

Lomita's storm drains flow mainly to Machado Lake and also to Los Angeles Harbor, and the storm drain system is separate from the sewer. The city says litter and debris clogs in storm drains and catch basins can cause flooding. Report a blocked grate before a storm, not after.

Sandbags are available at City Hall, 24300 Narbonne Ave., while supplies last, and the Tom Rico Center at 24428 Eshelman Ave. serves as a Red Cross shelter.

Storm water and your policy

Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood. Ask your insurer about separate flood coverage, which normally takes effect 30 days after purchase.

Owners, renters and who handles what

The Census QuickFacts owner-occupied rate for Lomita is 45.9 percent, so about 54 percent of households rent. Who can open a wall or file a claim differs by tenure.

If you rent, tell your landlord or property manager right away and photograph the damage before anything is moved. If you own, your policy is the document to read first.
