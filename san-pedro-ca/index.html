Wet months and dry months in San Pedro

We use no rainfall totals here, because we have no verified local measurements. What we can say is that the wet months bring roof, window and door leaks, and the dry months bring plumbing and appliance failures. Different sources call for different first steps. A ceiling left damp for days can also call for a mold remediation assessment.

When water is spreading in San Pedro

On a slope, water follows gravity down through floors, stairs and the lower unit. Move in this order.

Find the closest stop

Close the fixture valve or the main. If water seems to come from a roof or wall in a storm, tarp what you can reach safely and skip ladders in wind.

Protect the circuits

Switch off wet-room breakers only from a dry panel. If water drips through a light fixture or near an outlet, leave it alone and call the utility or 911.

Save the low rooms

Move what sits on lower floors, garages and stair landings, since runoff and drip pool there first. Furniture legs go up on blocks or foil.

Skip the sewage, ring us

Avoid water from drains or toilets. Then call (424) 550-5662, our 24/7 dispatch, and tell us the slope, floor level and what is wet.

Hillside and coastal facts that matter

San Pedro's own city plan records that a 1929 slide took homes at what was then the 600 block of Paseo del Mar, an area now called Sunken City that is geologically unsafe and closed to the public. In 2011, a landslide destroyed a 600-foot section of Paseo del Mar near White Point Nature Preserve.

We do not say what caused either event. We do say that if water is pooling against a retaining wall, a foundation edge or a slope behind your home, tell us and ask a geotechnical professional. Drying a room does not make ground stable.

The plan area also includes a San Pedro and Port of Los Angeles Dual Coastal Plan Zone. Ask the city whether coastal rules touch your repair before you rebuild.

Slides and your policy

Policies commonly exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow. Check your policy and ask your insurer before assuming a slope loss is covered.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency water removal available in San Pedro?

Yes. Call (424) 550-5662 at any hour of the day or night. We offer same-day response when available, though never an arrival time, and we ask about floor level, slope and the water source.

Do you cover every ZIP code in San Pedro?

We serve both street-delivery ZIPs, 90731 and 90732. ZIP 90731 also carries the Fort MacArthur and Terminal Island aliases. The remaining San Pedro ZIPs are PO Box only, so give your street address when you call.

Can heavy rain cause water damage I should report right away?

Yes. Roof, window and door leaks in the wet months can soak ceilings and walls fast. Photograph the area, tell your insurer promptly, and call us so drying begins before materials stay wet for days.

Does my historic or older San Pedro home dry differently?

Often, yes. Old plaster and trim are worth saving, so we measure moisture and remove only what has to go. Say if you are in Vinegar Hill or another historic area.

Is a slope or retaining wall problem part of water damage restoration?

No. We dry and repair the building. Ground movement is a question for a geotechnical professional, and your insurer can say how your policy treats it.
