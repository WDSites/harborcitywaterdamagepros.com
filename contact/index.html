What to have ready when you call

The dispatcher needs a few facts to plan the job. If you can answer them it speeds up the call, and if you cannot, say so and the dispatcher will work from what you know.

  1. The address and the nearest cross street.
  2. Where the water is coming from and whether it has been shut off.
  3. Which rooms and floors are wet, and for roughly how long.
  4. Whether the water looks clean, cloudy or like sewage.
  5. Whether any outlets, appliances or the panel are wet.
  6. Your insurer's name, if you have it, so the paperwork can be matched to a claim later.

What happens next

Once the dispatcher has the details you get plain advice on what to do while a crew is arranged. Same-day response is available when the schedule allows, but no arrival time is promised. If the situation involves electrical or gas risk, call the utility or 911 first.

On site the crew assesses the damage with moisture meters and thermal cameras and then explains the scope of work and how the price is set before work starts. See water mitigation for the drying stage and emergency water removal for standing water.

Crews are licensed and insured. For insurance questions, photos, moisture readings and a written scope are available to hand to your insurer, and we do not approve claims.
