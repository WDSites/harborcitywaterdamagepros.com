AC leak water damage repair is the drying and rebuilding of ceilings, insulation, closets and floors that were soaked by condensate overflow or a failed drain line from an air conditioner. The water is usually slow and quiet, so the first sign is often a stain and not a puddle.

Turn the system off at the thermostat to stop making more water, and set a towel or bucket under any drip. Our line at (424) 550-5662 runs 24/7 if the wet area is spreading.

Where air conditioner water comes from

Cooling coils pull moisture out of indoor air, and that water leaves through a condensate drain line. When the line clogs with algae and sludge, the drain pan under the coil fills and runs over. A float switch is meant to shut the unit off first, but switches fail or get bypassed.

The damage depends on where the air handler sits. In an attic it can soak the insulation and the ceiling board below. In a closet or garage it wets the floor, baseboards and the bottom of nearby walls.

A note on honesty for this area. Coastal Southern California homes vary a lot, and not every Harbor City home has central air. Many rely on wall units, window units or a mini-split, and those drip differently, usually onto a wall or an exterior surface. If you have no ducted system, the symptoms below may not apply to you, and we will say so on the phone.

AC leak safety: power, water and the attic

Switch the AC off at the thermostat first, then at the breaker if the panel is reachable and dry. Water near an air handler often sits close to its electrical connections, so do not touch the unit with wet hands or reach into a wet closet.

Do not enter an attic during a leak if the ceiling below is bulging. A dripping ceiling light means leaving the room, and any sparking, buzzing or burning smell goes to 911 or the utility first.

Who fixes what: HVAC technician first, then drying

We do not repair air conditioners. Cleaning the drain line, replacing a rusted pan, resetting a float switch and testing the system belong to an HVAC technician, and the leak will repeat until that is done. We handle the water damage side: extraction, drying and rebuilding.

If you rent, tell the landlord or property manager first. Fixing the unit may fall to them, and the damage claim may follow that same path.

Replacing a unit or rerouting plumbing for a drain may involve a permit. Ask LADBS whether the HVAC work needs a permit before the contractor starts. Your HVAC contractor can usually answer that too.

How we dry and repair after an AC leak

We check the area with a thermal imaging camera and pin and pinless moisture meters, starting at the air handler and working outward through ceilings, walls and floors. Readings are marked on a sketch so the same points can be rechecked on each visit.

Wet batt insulation comes out. Ceiling drywall that is soft, stained through or crumbling comes out too, while firm board that tests dry can often stay. Small areas of drywall are cut in straight lines so the patch is easy to rebuild.

For wet framing and subfloor, we set air movers aimed along the surface and an LGR dehumidifier sized to the room. An AC closet is small and tight, so we often open the baseboard or cut one or two access holes to move air through the wall cavity. The AC stays off while we dry, which makes the room warm, so tell us if anyone in the home depends on cooling.

After drying, we replace insulation, patch the ceiling, match the texture and prime and paint. The rebuild is part of water damage repair, and we explain it before work starts.

A clogged line that drips for months is a maintenance issue to most insurers, so read your policy before counting on a claim. To get a figure for the ceiling and insulation, call (424) 550-5662 and tell us where the stain is.

AC leaks and insurance: sudden or gradual

A condensate line that overflowed suddenly after a clog may be treated differently from a drip that ran for months, and policies word this differently. Ask your insurer how yours reads.

Keep every invoice from the HVAC visit and from our work, since the state insurance department says cleanup and repair cost records matter after water damage. For an AC leak, the HVAC invoice, our moisture logs and dated photos together make a file your insurer can review. Whether the claim is paid is your insurer's call, not ours.

Keeping the condensate line from leaking again

  • Ask your HVAC technician to flush the drain line at each service visit, since algae regrows.
  • Check that the float switch is wired in and working, and never bypass it.
  • Look at the drain pan during seasons you run the system heavily.
  • Do not ignore a small stain on a ceiling below an attic unit.
  • Keep a note of the date you first saw water, which helps with both repairs and a claim.

Frequently asked questions

Is AC leak water damage repair available in Harbor City?

Yes. Call (424) 550-5662 and describe where the air handler is and what is wet. The dispatch line takes calls at any hour, and a same-day visit is scheduled when crews are not already committed. We do not promise an arrival time.

There is a stain on my ceiling and the AC is in the attic. What should I do?

Turn the AC off at the thermostat and keep people out from under the stain. Take photos, then call an HVAC technician for the drain line and call us for the wet insulation and ceiling.

Can you fix the air conditioner itself?

No. We do not do HVAC repair, appliance repair or electrical work. We dry and rebuild what the water damaged, and an HVAC technician handles the clog, pan, float switch and unit.

My home has a wall unit, not central air. Can it still cause water damage?

Yes, but the pattern is different. Wall and window units usually drip onto a sill, a wall or the floor below, and the damage tends to be more local. A moisture reading will show how far it went.

Does a Harbor City AC repair need a permit?

It depends on the work. Your HVAC contractor should also confirm.

Is a slow AC drip covered by home insurance?

That depends on how your policy treats gradual leaks. Many policies exclude slow, long-term leaks while a sudden overflow may be treated differently. Check your policy and ask your insurer.
