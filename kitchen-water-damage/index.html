Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Harbor City, CA

We dry out cabinets, flooring and walls after sink, dishwasher or supply line leaks, then rebuild what could not be saved. Our dispatch line is open 24/7.

Kitchen water damage restoration is the process of removing water from a kitchen, drying the cabinets, floor and walls around it, and replacing the finishes that cannot be saved. Most losses start at the sink, the dishwasher or a supply line and spread under the cabinets where nobody can see.

Our dispatch line at (424) 550-5662 is open 24/7. While you wait, close the shutoff valve under the sink, move food and small appliances off the counters, and pull the dishwasher's plug only if the outlet is dry.

Where kitchen water goes once it leaves the pipe

A kitchen packs a lot of plumbing into a small footprint: a sink with two supply lines, a drain, a disposal, a dishwasher hose and often a refrigerator ice-maker line. Every one of the 9,912 housing units that Census Reporter counts in ZIP 90710 has a kitchen, and the layouts below show up in most of them.

Water that leaks under a sink spreads across the cabinet floor, then wicks into the toe kick and the subfloor. Because cabinets sit on top of the flooring and are boxed in, the water has few ways out. That is why a small drip can stay hidden for weeks before the floor softens.

What a wet kitchen shows you and what it means

Cabinet floor swollen or bubbling

Particle board has absorbed water. Once it swells, it rarely returns to its original shape.

Toe kick dark or peeling

Water reached the base of the cabinet and may be wicking into the wall behind it.

Laminate edges lifting

Water went in through the seams and the core is swelling. Laminate rarely recovers once the core has expanded.

Tile floor feels firm but grout is dark

Tile itself survives water, but the backer and subfloor below may be wet.

Meter and thermal readings sort cosmetic damage from deeper damage, and you see the numbers.

Safety steps for a leaking kitchen

Kitchens combine water with outlets, appliances and often gas, so slow down before you start wiping.

  • Shut off the stop valve under the sink, or the main water valve if the stop is stuck.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.
  • Do not touch the dishwasher, refrigerator or range with wet hands or while standing in water.
  • Turn off power to the kitchen circuits only if the panel is reachable dry. If water has reached outlets, call 911 or the utility.
  • Call a plumber for the leaking part. We dry and rebuild, but we do not repair plumbing or appliances.

How we dry under and behind cabinets

Standing water comes out first with extraction units, and we lift the toe kick to expose the cabinet base. Surface fans cannot reach a closed cabinet, so we drill small ports in the toe kick or the cabinet back and push dry air in through an injectidry system. A thermal imaging camera and moisture meter then show whether the box, the wall and the subfloor are drying.

LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room while air movers keep it moving over wet surfaces. Where mats are an option, they draw water from under finished flooring, which can save floors that would otherwise come up. Typical drying takes several days, and the readings tell us when it is complete.

What we keep, what we replace and what stays yours to decide

Solid wood cabinet doors and boxes often dry out, but particle board boxes and laminate faces tend to swell and delaminate. Countertops usually stay in place if the cabinet below is dry and level, but we may detach them to reach wet material. Vinyl, tile and engineered flooring each have separate tests, and Category 3 water pushes porous materials toward replacement.

We explain every keep-or-replace call and show the readings, and the decision remains yours. We rebuild drywall, paint, flooring and cabinet boxes, but a custom or specialty cabinet may need the original cabinet maker. For work of $500 or more, the Contractors State License Board says the contractor must be licensed, so confirm that before you sign anything.

Plumber, restoration crew and insurer: who does what

The plumber fixes the pipe, fitting or hose and we handle the water damage that followed. Replacing supply lines or relocating pipes is plumbing work, and LADBS says its permits cover potable water and drainage piping, so ask LADBS whether the repair needs a permit. Kitchen plumbing changes may need a permit, so ask LADBS before cabinet or sink work.

Sudden and accidental leaks are often covered by homeowners policies, and gradual leaks often are not. Keep every receipt from the cleanup and the cabinet work, as the state insurance department recommends. The loss record from the kitchen is yours to send in, and approval is for your insurer to decide.

Mistakes to avoid and how to prevent the next leak

  • Do not seal wet cabinets with new caulk, paint or flooring until readings are down.
  • Do not leave a slow drip under the sink for weeks, because the toe kick hides the damage.
  • Do not run the dishwasher again until the plumber clears it.
  • Replace old braided supply hoses and check the dishwasher and ice-maker lines once a year.
  • Keep a flashlight and a small bowl in the sink cabinet for a monthly look.

Frequently asked questions

Is kitchen water damage restoration available in Harbor City?

Yes. Kitchen leaks go to the same night-and-day line at (424) 550-5662, and same-day service depends on the schedule. No arrival time is quoted, but the scope and price are set out before any cabinet is touched.

My cabinets are swollen at the bottom. Can they be saved?

It depends on the material. Solid wood boxes often dry, while particle board that has swollen is usually replaced. A moisture reading and a look at the cabinet floor tell us which one you have.

The floor near my sink feels soft. What is going on?

Soft flooring usually means water has been under it for a while, often from a slow drip. Call a plumber to stop the leak, then call us to check the subfloor and cabinet base for moisture.

Do I need to remove the countertop to dry the cabinets?

Usually not. We dry through the toe kick or small ports and only detach a counter when wet material below cannot be reached any other way.

Will insurance cover a leaking dishwasher?

That depends on your policy and how the leak happened. A sink line that fails at once is judged differently from a drip behind the cabinet, so keep photos and receipts either way.

Do older Harbor City kitchens have more leak risk?

Not necessarily, but old supply hoses and shutoff valves wear out in any home. Check your hoses and valves and replace anything cracked or stiff.
