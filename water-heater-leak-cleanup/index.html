Water heater leak cleanup is the removal and drying work that follows when a tank, fitting or relief valve lets water out onto the surrounding floor and walls. Harbor City calls reach a live dispatcher at (424) 550-5662 day or night.

While you wait, close the cold water supply valve above the heater and set the heater to off at its gas control or breaker. Keep people and pets away from hot puddles, and move boxes and stored items up off the wet floor.

Tank failure, loose fitting or slow corrosion: three kinds of heater leak

A tank that has rusted through is the heavy case. Once the steel lining fails, the whole tank can empty, and a typical 40 to 50 gallon tank is a lot of water in a small footprint. That heater is finished and a plumber replaces it.

Fittings and valves are the small case. A drip from the supply connection, the drain valve or the temperature and pressure relief valve may come from a part that can be replaced without a new tank. Those leaks often run slowly and wet a small patch of floor for days.

Slow corrosion sits between the two. Rust streaks at the base, a pooled film under the tank and a damp wall behind it show a leak that has been going for a while. Wet drywall that old can already have soft paper and stained framing, so we take readings before we decide how much to open.

Garage and closet installs make the damage worse

Southern California homes often put the heater in a garage corner, a hall closet or a utility room. Those spots are convenient for the plumbing but awkward when water escapes. Many have no floor drain, so water spreads under the heater and creeps toward the nearest wall, stored boxes and the door sill.

Closet installs are tougher still. Water slides under the closet door into a hallway or bedroom, and it wicks into the carpet pad and baseboards of the next room. Garage slabs are concrete and dry fast, but the drywall at the heater and anything stored on cardboard on the floor do not.

Census Reporter's ACS profile counts roughly 9,900 housing units in ZIP 90710, and almost every one has a water heater somewhere in a garage, closet or utility space. Each home keeps its heater somewhere, and where yours sits tells us in advance which walls and floors are likely wet, so describe the location when you call.

Safety at a leaking water heater: gas, electric and hot water

A heater combines water, fuel and power, so the order of steps matters more here than with most leaks. These four cards cover what to do before anyone mops.

Smell gas, leave

If you smell gas, go outside and call 911 or your gas utility from there. Do not flip switches or look for the shutoff in the house.

Cold supply off

The cold inlet valve above the heater cuts the feed. Close it clockwise. Hot water in the puddle can scald, so keep hands out of it.

Heater power off

Turn a gas control to off or switch an electric heater's breaker off, only from dry footing. An electric element sitting in water is a shock risk.

How we dry a heater leak

Extraction first. We pull standing water with extraction units, then work along the bottom of the wall and the edge of any carpet to lift trapped water. In a closet, we pull the heater's surround and any stored items clear so the floor is open.

Then comes the drying system. Air movers are aimed along the wet baseboard, and small holes or a removed baseboard let air reach the bottom of the cavity. An LGR dehumidifier runs in the room, and where water reached flooring in the next room, mat or injectidry systems pull air through the layers. HEPA air scrubbers join when demolition raises dust.

Wet batt insulation and swollen drywall are the usual removals. We map readings on a sketch, return to log them and remove equipment when the materials meet the dry reference set from an unaffected area, which typically takes about three to five days.

Heater leaks, flood coverage and your policy

Water from a failed tank counts as water from a source inside the home. The state insurance department's flood guide says flood policies do not usually cover moisture damage that could have been avoided, which is why a slowly weeping tank matters to a claim. So a heater leak is a homeowners policy question, not a flood question.

Gradual leaks are the weak spot in many policies. If the damage was visible for a long time before it was reported, ask your insurer how that is treated. Photograph the heater, the label and the wet areas before we begin, and keep the plumber's invoice.

Mistakes to avoid after a heater leak

  • Running the heater with the tank empty. A gas burner or electric element can be damaged when it fires with no water inside.
  • Leaving a wet cardboard storage pile in the garage. It wicks water and holds it against drywall.
  • Replacing only the heater and ignoring the wall behind it. New plumbing does not dry old drywall.
  • Assuming no permit is needed. LADBS lists permits for fuel gas and potable water piping, but its plumbing page does not mention water heaters, so ask LADBS whether your heater replacement needs a permit.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency water heater leak cleanup available in Harbor City?

Yes. Dial (424) 550-5662 any hour, including weekends and holidays, and a dispatcher answers. A same-day visit is possible on many days, and the dispatcher will say plainly when it is not. Tell us where the heater is, whether the cold supply is closed and which rooms are wet.

My water heater is dripping from the side pipe. Is that a leak I should worry about?

It can be. A pipe near the relief valve that drips often means the valve is discharging, and a plumber should look at it. Even a slow drip can wet a wall over weeks, so check the baseboard and floor beside the heater.

How much water can a tank release?

A 40 to 50 gallon tank can hold that much, and a full failure can spill most of it. Most of that water ends up spreading across the floor in minutes, which is why extraction comes first.

Do you replace or repair the water heater?

No. We do not do plumbing repair or appliance repair. A plumber replaces or repairs the heater. We extract, dry, clean and rebuild drywall, flooring and paint, and we coordinate timing with your plumber when needed.

The leak was in my garage and the floor looks dry now. Do I still need drying?

Often yes. Concrete dries on its own, but the drywall at the heater wall, the bottom plate and anything stored there may not. A moisture reading tells you whether the wall behind the heater needs attention.
