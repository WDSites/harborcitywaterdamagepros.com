When water is spreading in Torrance

At 20.52 square miles, the city cannot be reached by one number for every problem. Work through the four moves below, then phone us.

Stop what feeds it

Close the fixture valve or the main at your house. If the leak is a main line outside, report it to your provider, since Torrance has more than one.

Dry power first

Leave breakers alone if you would wade to reach them. A dry, reachable panel can be switched off for wet circuits, and anything near standing water waits for an electrician or the utility.

Clear a path

Pull rugs, boxes and furniture off wet floors and prop up upholstered pieces on foil or blocks. Open space lets extraction units work without moving your belongings twice.

Avoid dirty water, then dial

Do not wade through water from a toilet, drain or outdoor overflow. Call (424) 550-5662, our 24/7 dispatch, and we will plan around what you describe.

Sewer backups: 287 miles of city lines

The city maintains 287 miles of sewer lines and 9 lift stations. Report a backup through the city's online service request or Public Works at 310-781-6900. The city page lists no separate after-hours sewer number.

A sewage backup is a Category 3 loss. Stay out of the room, and do not run drains. Once the line is cleared, our water damage cleanup crews remove porous materials and disinfect what remains, following ANSI/IICRC S500 practice.

Electrical and gas safety

If water has reached outlets, an electrical panel or a gas appliance, stay out. If anything smells like gas or hums, that call goes to 911 or the utility, not to us.

Street flooding, sandbags and the city yard

The city lists localized street and intersection flooding as a possible storm impact. For non-emergency flooding or drainage, call Public Works at 310-781-6900. Sandbags are self-fill at the City Yard and at any city fire station, so you can prepare a garage door or low entry before rain.

We do not state flood zones, so use the FEMA flood map to see your own. Standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood, so ask about separate coverage. Our flood damage restoration page describes what drying looks like after storm water.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency water removal available in Torrance?

Yes, the line at (424) 550-5662 is staffed through nights, weekends and holidays. Tell the dispatcher where the water came from and how far it has gone. We try for same-day service where schedules allow but never quote an arrival time.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Torrance?

We serve the six street-delivery ZIP codes, 90501, 90502, 90503, 90504, 90505 and 90506. ZIP 90503 holds City Hall at 3031 Torrance Blvd., and 90506 has no listed population. If your mail says Redondo Beach but you live in western Torrance, which uses ZIP 90277, tell us your address and we will confirm.

Does the city or Cal Water provide my water?

Torrance Municipal Water serves about 78 percent of the city, and Cal Water covers portions through its Dominguez and Hermosa-Redondo systems. Your bill names the provider, so check it before you report a main leak.

Who do I call for a sewer backup in Torrance?

Report it through the city's online service request or to Public Works at 310-781-6900. The city maintains the sewer lines, so a backup in the main is theirs to clear. Stay out of the affected area until it is.

Where do I get sandbags in Torrance?

The city says sandbags are available at the City Yard and at any city fire station. They are self-fill, so bring a shovel and gloves.
