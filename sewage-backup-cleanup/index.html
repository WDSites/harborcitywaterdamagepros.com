Sewage backup is contaminated water that calls for protective gear and removal of porous materials. Our Harbor City dispatch line is open 24/7.

Sewage backup cleanup is the removal of contaminated wastewater that has come up through a drain, toilet or floor fixture, followed by cleaning and drying of everything it touched. The Harbor City line, (424) 550-5662, is answered around the clock.

While you wait, keep people and pets out of the affected rooms, stop using toilets, sinks and washing machines, and do not try to clean it yourself. Call a plumber about the blockage, because we handle the contamination and not the line.

Why sewage water is treated differently from other water

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is a published reference that sorts water into three categories. Category 1 is clean supply water, Category 2 is used water such as washing machine discharge, and Category 3 is grossly contaminated water that includes sewage.

Category 3 water can carry bacteria and other contaminants, so we treat anything it touched as a removal and disinfection job and not a dry-and-go job. That is why the usual shortcuts, such as a box fan and a mop, are not a fit here.

Porous materials, including carpet, pad, drywall and insulation, absorb contaminated water and cannot be reliably cleaned afterward. Those materials normally come out, while hard surfaces like tile and sealed concrete can be cleaned and disinfected.

What a sewage backup looks like in a house

Toilet or tub gurgling

Bubbling or slow draining across several fixtures at once suggests a blockage lower in the line.

Water rising in a floor drain

Gray or dark water coming up a floor drain, laundry standpipe or lowest tub means the line is backing up.

Foul odor with no visible water

A strong sewer smell can mean contamination has soaked flooring or a cavity out of sight.

Several drains failing together

When every fixture slows down at once, the problem is usually the main line and not a single trap.

Dark stains on baseboards

A tide line on walls shows how high the water rose, and tells us how far to cut.

First-hour safety steps for a sewage backup

This is the one water loss where we ask you to stay completely out. Do not wade through it, do not use a household wet vacuum on it, and do not flush or run water while the line is blocked. If the sewage reaches outlets or the panel, the utility or 911 is the first call, not us.

Keep everyone clear

Keep children, pets and anyone with a health concern out of the affected rooms. Close doors to limit the spread through the house.

Stop adding water

Do not flush, run the dishwasher, wash clothes or use sinks. More water only pushes more sewage up the line.

Skip the DIY cleanup

A household wet vacuum can spray contaminated mist and the machine itself becomes contaminated. Leave extraction to crews with proper equipment.

Call a plumber, then us

A plumber must clear the blockage. Call (424) 550-5662 so extraction and cleaning can be planned alongside.

Whose line is it, and who do you call

A blockage can sit in the pipe serving your building or farther along in the city main, and the answer decides who clears it. LA Sanitation and Environment lists 1-800-773-CITY (2489) and the 311 line for city service requests, so call them to report the problem and ask where responsibility lies.

A plumber can check your own lateral, and a camera inspection often shows where the stoppage is. Pipe repairs on a drain line are permit questions for LADBS, whose plumbing page covers drainage, waste and vent work.

We start cleaning once the water has stopped rising, or sooner if the plumber has made it safe for us to begin.

How we clean up after sewage

Crews wear protective gear that suits Category 3 work, including gloves, boots and respirators. Standing water comes out with extraction units, and the affected area is sealed off from the rest of the house with plastic barriers where we can.

Carpet, pad, saturated drywall and insulation that touched the water are removed and bagged. Meter readings and thermal images show how far sewage wicked into the walls, so cut lines follow data.

Remaining surfaces are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial product. Then air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and a HEPA air scrubber dry and clean the air in the sealed area until readings return to a dry reference.

We then rebuild finishes if you want us to, including drywall, flooring and paint, and we do not do plumbing repair or mold testing labs.

How insurance can treat sewage backup

Many standard homeowners policies treat sewer or drain backup as a separate item, sometimes an add-on endorsement. Check your policy for wording on backup of sewers or drains, and ask your insurer before work starts.

Sewer backup coverage varies by policy, so ask your insurer directly, and note that the state insurance department says standard homeowners policies leave flood out. A sewage backup does not fit neatly into either box, so your policy language matters.

Take photos before anything is removed, and keep plumber reports and receipts. Our photos, readings and scope can go to your insurer, and whether a sewage loss is covered depends on your policy and the cause.

Storm context, mistakes and prevention

LA Sanitation notes that the Machado Lake rehabilitation project in Harbor City includes treatment systems at major storm drain inlets. Storm drains and sewers are separate systems, but heavy rain can still strain lines, so a clog that shows up in wet months is worth reporting quickly.

Once the line is clear, water damage cleanup covers drying and rebuilding what remains. Prevention is simple. Keep grease, wipes and paper towels out of drains, and have a plumber camera the lateral if backups repeat.

  • Do not use chemical drain openers on a full line, because the standing water becomes more hazardous.
  • Do not walk through the water and track it into clean rooms.
  • Do not keep soaked carpet to dry it later.
  • Do not ignore a second backup, which points to a deeper blockage.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency sewage backup cleanup available in Harbor City?

Yes. Someone answers at (424) 550-5662 at every hour, and same-day response is offered based on the schedule, never as a set time. No arrival time is quoted, and until crews come you should keep everyone out of the affected area.

Sewage is coming up from my floor drain. What should I do first?

Keep people and pets away, stop using water in the house and do not flush. Call a plumber for the blockage and call us about the cleanup. Do not use a household wet vacuum on it.

Is it Category 3 water?

Yes. Under the S500 reference, sewage is Category 3 contaminated water. That is why porous materials it touches are normally removed rather than dried.

Who do I call, the city or a plumber?

Both can matter. LA Sanitation and Environment lists 1-800-773-CITY (2489) and 311, and a plumber can check the line serving your building.

Can the carpet be saved after a sewage backup?

Usually not. Carpet and pad absorb contaminated water and cannot be reliably cleaned, so they normally come out. Hard sealed floors can often be cleaned and disinfected.

Does a Harbor City storm make sewage backups more likely?

Storm drains and sewers are separate, but wet months can strain lines. If backups repeat after rain, report it through the city numbers and have a plumber inspect your lateral.
