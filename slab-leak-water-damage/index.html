Slab leak water damage begins when a pressurized water line under or inside a concrete foundation slab breaks and the water finds its way up into flooring, baseboards and the bottom of the walls. Call (424) 550-5662 any time of day, because a slab leak does not wait for business hours.

While you wait, close the main water valve at the house and see whether the meter stops moving. Keep shoes off damp floors, lift rugs and furniture off the wet area, and call a plumber for the pipe, because we dry and rebuild but do not locate or repair lines.

How a pipe under concrete ends up wetting your floor

Most homes built on a concrete slab run supply lines either through the slab or just beneath it, while drain lines sit lower. A supply line under the slab stays pressurized all day, so even a pinhole keeps pushing water outward until it finds a crack, a joint or the edge of the slab.

Slab-on-grade foundations are common across Southern California in general, but we do not claim any share for Harbor City, and many homes here sit on raised foundations instead. If your house has a crawl space, the page on emergency water removal covers standing water that has no slab to climb through.

The water usually travels sideways before it surfaces. It can wet the subfloor, the bottom plate of a wall and the back of a baseboard well before a stain appears, which is why a slab leak often looks small on the day it is found.

Signs the water is coming from the slab, and what each one means

A warm spot on the floor

A hot water line under the slab can warm the surface above it. A warm patch on tile or laminate that stays warm with every fixture off points toward a leak in the hot line.

Hissing or running sound

Listen with the house quiet and every tap closed. A steady hiss in a wall or floor means pressurized water is escaping somewhere.

A water meter that keeps moving

With every fixture off, a moving meter dial says water is leaving the system. The leak may be under the slab or elsewhere, and a plumber has to sort that out.

Damp or buckled flooring

Laminate that lifts, tile grout that darkens and carpet that stays damp from underneath all suggest moisture rising from the slab.

Musty smell near baseboards

Odor with no visible water usually means a wet subfloor or wall cavity. A moisture meter can map where it ends.

A water bill that jumped

A bill far above your normal pattern, with no new habits to explain it, is worth a plumber's call.

First-hour safety steps for a suspected slab leak

Water and electricity do not mix, so look at the floor before you touch anything. Water near outlets, a panel or appliances is a call to 911 or the electric utility first and to us second.

Close the main valve

Turn the house main shutoff fully closed, then check whether the meter dial stops. If it does not stop, tell the plumber and us on the phone.

Cut power only if it is dry

A damp floor between you and the panel means the breaker waits for an electrician. Otherwise leave it and call the utility.

Protect what you can lift

Move rugs, shoes, boxes and furniture off wet flooring. Place foil or plastic under wooden legs that must stay.

Call the plumber, then the line

A licensed plumber has to find and fix the pipe. Call (424) 550-5662 as well, so drying can start while the pipe work is scheduled.

If the leak looks like it is on the street side of the meter, call the water utility, and the line to use is on the LADWP news site. Ask your water utility where its responsibility ends and yours begins, since that decides who pays for the repair.

How we dry flooring and walls after the pipe is stopped

Once the line is shut off or repaired, we start with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera. The camera shows cool, wet patches across the floor and up the walls, and the meters confirm the readings at each spot we mark.

Standing water comes out with extraction units. Wet laminate, vinyl plank and carpet pad are usually pulled because they hold water against the slab, while tile can often stay if the grout and the bond are intact.

Water trapped under flooring needs a different drying path than a wet wall. We use mat or injection systems that push dry air under hardwood or through a cut-out section, and LGR dehumidifiers with air movers handle the room. A written moisture log tracks each wall and floor reading until it matches a dry reference.

Baseboards come off so the bottom few inches of drywall can breathe. If insulation or drywall has stayed wet from the bottom up, we cut it at a clean line, often a foot or two above the highest reading, rather than guessing.

Where slab leak damage meets your insurance policy

A pipe under the slab is a plumbing loss and not a flood loss according to the state insurance department's flood guide, so it is a question for your homeowner policy.

A leak that has run slowly for months may be treated as gradual, and gradual damage is often excluded. Check your policy for wording about plumbing leaks and about the cost of reaching the pipe, which can differ from the cost of the damage.

Take photos of the floor, the meter and any plumber report before anything is removed. Photos, readings and a written scope can go to your insurer, though coverage and payout are theirs to decide.

Who does what, and mistakes that make a slab leak worse

The plumber finds the leak and chooses between a reroute, a spot repair or lining the pipe. LADBS lists plumbing permits that cover modifications to potable water piping, so ask LADBS whether the pipe work needs one, and CSLB says any job of $500 or more needs a licensed contractor.

For a repipe or a slab tunnel job, get the plumber's license number from the state board and the payment schedule in writing. Apply that limit to the plumber and to anyone who rebuilds your floors.

  • Do not run a space heater or fan over a warm spot and call the problem solved.
  • Do not drill or cut the slab yourself to chase the sound.
  • Do not leave wet carpet pad down because the top feels dry.
  • Do not wait on a slow meter, because the line keeps running while you decide.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency slab leak water damage cleanup available in Harbor City?

Yes. Calls to (424) 550-5662 are answered at any hour, and a same-day visit can be arranged depending on the day's load. Ask the dispatcher what is realistic for your address, since no arrival time is promised.

My floor feels warm and I hear hissing. Is that a slab leak?

Those are classic signs of a pressurized line leaking under the floor, especially a hot water line. Close the main valve, check whether the meter stops, and call a plumber to confirm. We handle the wet flooring and walls once the pipe is dealt with.

Do you find and fix the leaking pipe?

No. Leak detection, rerouting and pipe lining are plumber work. We extract water, dry the floor, subfloor and walls, remove damaged materials and rebuild finishes.

Can wet flooring over a slab be saved?

Sometimes. Tile and sealed floors can often be dried in place, while laminate, vinyl plank and carpet pad are usually removed. Moisture readings decide it for each room.

Does homeowners insurance cover slab leaks?

A slab leak that went unnoticed for a long time is judged differently from one found quickly, and the policy wording decides. Ask your insurer, and note that the California Department of Insurance describes flood policies as excluding water from sources inside the home.

Do slab leaks happen in Harbor City homes with raised foundations?

A true slab leak needs a slab, so a raised-foundation home with a crawl space has a different problem, with plumbing exposed below the floor. Check what your house sits on, and ask a plumber which applies. Water under a raised floor is covered on our crawl space page.
